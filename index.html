ERC-7540 vaults can use a separate request-and-claim flow. If you know your public request controller and exact request ID, this optional inspection checks ERC-165 interface declarations, then reads pending and claimable redemption shares at one verified block. Important limits: ERC-7540 status functions return SHARE quantities, not guaranteed withdrawable asset amounts. The async redemption interface must be declared by ERC-165; absent or malformed responses are NOT zero balances. Both pending and claimable can read zero for a wrong ID, another controller, an already claimed request, or a protocol-specific reason. Even a positive claimable figure is not proof of a successful transaction, vault solvency, or a redeemable asset valuation. Some vaults are not ERC-7540 compliant; use their official documentation. WHAT YOU ARE ACTUALLY CHECKING

A limit, not a promise.

maxWithdraw(owner) is the standard's declared asset amount allowed through a direct withdrawal. maxRedeem(owner) is the declared redeemable share amount. previewRedeem(shares) estimates assets at the sampled block but intentionally does not enforce withdrawal limits.

Results can become stale before you transact. Some vaults impose asynchronous request/claim flows, pause permissions, permissioned exits, share locks, off-chain intermediaries, cooldowns, or liquidity restrictions. ERC-4626 reads cannot prove any of these are safe or that an exit will execute. Inspect protocol terms, implementation and the current withdrawal queue separately.
